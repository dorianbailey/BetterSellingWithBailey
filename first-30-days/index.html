The first 30 days after a parent passes

A lot lands on you at once. This is the order I'd tackle the house and the paperwork in, written for families in Erie County. Print it, or have it emailed to you so it's there when you need it.

The first few days

  • Lock up the house and gather every key. Write down who else has one.
  • Keep the heat and utilities on. An empty house in an Erie winter can freeze its pipes.
  • Call the homeowner's insurance company. Tell them the house is empty and ask whether that changes the coverage.
  • Ask the funeral director for certified copies of the death certificate. Banks, insurers and the Register of Wills may each want one.
  • Don't throw anything away or give things away yet. Valuables turn up in coat pockets, books and coffee cans, and things that look like junk are often what collectors want.

Weeks 1 and 2: the paperwork

  • Find the will and the important papers: deeds, vehicle titles, insurance policies, bank and retirement statements, and recent tax returns. Keep them together in one box.
  • Work out who can sign. In Pennsylvania the Register of Wills gives the executor the authority to act, called letters testamentary when there's a will and letters of administration when there isn't. In Erie County that office is in Room 122 of the Erie County Courthouse.
  • Talk to an estate attorney about probate, deadlines and what can be sold before the estate is settled.
  • Notify Social Security, any pension, the banks and the credit card companies.
  • Have the mail forwarded so bills and statements don't get missed.
  • Mark the inheritance tax dates. Pennsylvania inheritance tax is due within nine months of the death, and paying within three months earns a 5% discount. Ask the attorney or an accountant how it applies.

Weeks 3 and 4: the house

  • Photograph every room before anything moves. It helps with the estate's records, and with remembering what was where.
  • Let the family choose keepsakes first, and write down who is taking what. Agree on one point person for decisions.
  • Don't sell the standout pieces early. The truck, the tools or the collection are what bring buyers to an estate sale, and they help everything else sell.
  • Ask the realtor when the house needs to be empty, and plan backward from that date.
  • Get an honest estimate for the contents. A free estate sale walkthrough tells you what the sale could bring, and whether a sale makes sense at all.

This is a general guide, not legal or tax advice. An estate attorney can tell you exactly what applies to your family and your parent's estate.
