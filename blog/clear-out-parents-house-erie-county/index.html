How to clear out a parent's house in Erie County

A step-by-step plan for families: what to do first, what not to throw away, and how to get the house ready to sell.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 9, 2026About 7 minutes to read

Clearing out a parent's house is one of the hardest jobs a family takes on. It's emotional, it's physical, and it usually comes with a deadline: a listing date, a closing, or a move-in day at a senior community. After running sales in homes across Erie County, this is the order I'd recommend.

1. Don't throw anything away yet

The first instinct is to start filling trash bags. Wait. Valuable things hide in ordinary places: jewelry in coat pockets, cash tucked in books, old coins in a coffee can that turn out to be silver. Things that look like junk to the family, like a box of old toys, a tackle box or a shelf of records, are often what collectors come for.

Set up one box for paperwork you find along the way: the will, titles, deeds, insurance policies, bank and tax records. The executor or the estate's attorney will want them.

2. Figure out who can sign

If your parent has passed away, the executor named in the will usually handles the belongings. If your parent is alive but has moved into care, it's typically whoever holds power of attorney. An estate sale company needs that person to sign the contract. If you're not sure who has authority, ask the estate's attorney before anything is sold.

3. Let the family choose first

Give everyone a chance to claim keepsakes before anything is priced. Agree on one point person to make decisions, and write down who is taking what. It prevents hard feelings later, and it means the sale is planned around what's actually for sale.

4. Don't sell the big pieces early

It's tempting to sell the truck, the tools or the collection first to get some money in hand. Those standout pieces are what bring buyers to an estate sale, and they help everything else sell. If you're weighing it, ask whoever runs your sale before you list anything on your own.

5. Decide how you'll sell it

Most families choose between three options:

  • An estate sale. The contents are priced and sold in the home over one to three days. It usually brings in the most money for a full house.
  • A cleanout crew. The fastest way to an empty house, but you pay them and get nothing back for the contents.
  • An auction. Best for a handful of high-value or specialty pieces that draw bidders from far away.

Many families combine them: an estate sale for the contents, then removal of whatever is left.

6. Work backward from your deadline

If the house is going on the market, ask your realtor when it needs to be empty and plan from there. I hold most sales inside the home, so an Erie winter doesn't get in the way. Keep in mind that good estate sale companies book up, especially for spring and summer dates, so call early.

7. Sale weekend

You don't need to be there, and many families prefer not to be. Prices usually start firm and become more flexible later in the sale, which is normal and helps clear more of the house.

8. After the sale

Once the sale ends, you should be paid and the leftovers handled: kept, donated or removed. Ask before you sign whether removal is included and what it costs. With me, leftovers are removed free, and hauling away large furniture is an optional $295 you can decide on at the end of the sale.

Quick checklist

  • Hold off on throwing things away
  • Start a box for important papers
  • Confirm who has authority to sign
  • Let family claim keepsakes, and write it down
  • Keep the standout pieces for the sale
  • Set the plan around your listing or move-in date

Common questions

How long does it take to clear out a house?

It depends on the size of the house and how much is in it. Plan backward from your deadline, and book an estate sale company early, since good dates fill up.

Should we hold the sale ourselves?

You can, but pricing, advertising, staffing and cleanup take far more time than most families expect. A professional sale usually frees up your time and helps the sale bring in more.

What if we live out of town?

That's common. With a managed estate sale you don't need to be there for setup or the sale. I pay clients as soon as the sale ends.
