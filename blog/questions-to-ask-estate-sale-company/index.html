5 questions to ask before you hire an estate sale company

What to ask, what a good answer sounds like, and how I answer each one.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 11, 2026About 4 minutes to read

Hiring an estate sale company means handing a stranger the keys to a house full of someone's life. A good company answers these five questions clearly, before you sign anything. Save this list and bring it to every walkthrough, including mine.

1. Are you insured?

Look for: a clear yes. Strangers will be walking through the house, and things can get bumped or broken.

My answer: Yes. Every sale is covered by my business insurance.

2. Will I get a written contract?

Look for: fees and dates in writing, before anything happens.

My answer: Always. Everything is in writing first.

3. When do I get paid?

Look for: a set day, not "after we finish up."

My answer: The day the sale ends, with a written statement of what sold and what was deducted. Here's what that statement looks like, line by line.

4. What happens to the leftovers?

Look for: a plan and a price, up front. Some companies charge extra for cleanout, so ask what removal costs before you sign.

My answer: Leftovers are kept, donated or removed free, your choice. Hauling away large furniture is an optional $295, and you can decide after the sale.

5. What will it cost, all in?

Look for: one clear number, with no surprise fees.

My answer: A flat $995 plus 25% of sales. The $500 deposit that holds your dates counts toward it, and the rest comes out of the proceeds. See what estate sale companies in Erie charge for a side-by-side comparison.

A few more worth asking

  • How do you price items? I research every item using recent sold listings, not asking prices. Here's how.
  • How do you advertise? I promote every sale on multiple platforms and to my sale-alert list.
  • Who runs the sale? My helpers and I. You work with me directly from the walkthrough to the last day.
  • Can I see past sales and reviews? Here are my past sales and my Google reviews.

Common questions

What should I ask an estate sale company before hiring?

Ask if they're insured, whether you'll get a written contract, when you'll be paid, what happens to the leftovers, and what it will cost all in.

Should an estate sale company be insured?

Yes. Shoppers will be walking through the home, so the company should carry business insurance. Mine covers every sale.

When should an estate sale company pay you?

On a set day you agree to in writing. I pay clients the day the sale ends, with a written statement.
