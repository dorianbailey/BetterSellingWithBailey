What not to throw away before an estate sale

What sells better than you think, where valuables hide, and the papers to keep.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 14, 2026About 3 minutes to read

Short answer: don't throw anything away until someone who prices estate sales has walked through the house. Things that look like junk are often what collectors come for, valuables turn up in odd places, and the paperwork the executor needs is easy to toss by mistake.

Things that sell better than you'd think

These are the kinds of things that do well at the sales I run in Erie County:

  • Tools, from the workbench, the basement and the garage
  • Collectibles and toys: LEGO, Star Wars, sports and trading cards, video games
  • Hunting and fishing gear
  • Harley-Davidson clothing and merchandise
  • Vintage clothing
  • Antiques, and the everyday household goods people furnish their homes with

More detail in what sells at an estate sale, ranked.

Places valuables hide

Before anything goes in a bag, check coat pockets, books and coffee cans. Cash, jewelry and papers turn up in all three.

Paperwork to keep

Keep every paper until the executor has gone through it. The ones that matter most:

  • The will
  • Deeds and vehicle titles
  • Insurance policies
  • Bank and retirement statements
  • Recent tax returns

Keep them together in one box. If you're the executor, here's what Pennsylvania asks of you for the house contents.

Don't sell or give away the best pieces early

The truck, the tools or the collection are what bring buyers to an estate sale, and they help everything else sell. Let the family choose keepsakes first, write down who takes what, and leave the rest for the walkthrough.

Common questions

Should I throw things away before an estate sale?

No. Wait until someone who prices estate sales has walked through. Things that look like junk are often what collectors want, and valuables turn up in coat pockets, books and coffee cans.

What papers should I keep when clearing out a parent's house?

Keep every paper until the executor has gone through it, especially the will, deeds, vehicle titles, insurance policies, bank and retirement statements, and recent tax returns.
