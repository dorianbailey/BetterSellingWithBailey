3 mistakes families make clearing out a parent's house

And what to do instead. A short guide for Erie County families.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 11, 2026About 3 minutes to read

When a parent passes or moves into care, the instinct is to start clearing right away. It's understandable, and it's where the most expensive mistakes happen. Here are the three I see most, and what to do instead.

1. Throwing things out too soon

Valuables turn up in coat pockets, books and coffee cans. And what looks like junk is often exactly what collectors want: old toys in the attic, tools in the basement, holiday decorations in the garage.

Instead: hold off on the dumpster until someone who knows what sells has walked through the house. Check pockets, drawers, books and boxes as you go, and set aside anything that looks like paperwork. If you want a second set of eyes, the walkthrough is free.

2. Selling the best pieces first

It's tempting to sell the truck, the tools or the collection on your own to "get something out of it." But those are the items that bring buyers to the sale, and once buyers are there, they buy everything else too.

Instead: keep the headliners for the sale and let them do the work of drawing a crowd. Family keepsakes are different: take what the family wants to keep before the sale.

3. Turning off the heat

An empty house in an Erie winter can freeze its pipes, and a burst pipe can damage the house and everything in it.

Instead: keep the utilities on, and tell the insurance company the house is empty. Ask how they handle a vacant home, and have someone check on it regularly.

Common questions

Should I throw things away before an estate sale?

No. Wait until someone who knows what sells has walked through the house. Valuables and collectibles often hide in places that look like junk.

Should I keep the utilities on in an empty house?

Yes, especially in winter. An empty house in Erie can freeze its pipes. Keep the heat on and tell your insurance company the house is empty.
