How you get paid after an estate sale, line by line

An example sale-day statement from start to finish: what sold, my fee, the deposit, and what you're paid the day the sale ends.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 11, 2026About 3 minutes to read

One of the first questions families ask is "when do I get paid?" With some companies, the answer is weeks after the sale. With me, it's the day the sale ends, along with a written statement of what sold and what was deducted. Here's an example, line by line.

On sale day

My helpers and I run the sale. Advertising, my sale-alert list, every shopper, checkout and payments are all included. Your time there: zero hours, unless you want to stop by.

That's $10,000 in sales, minus the $3,495 total fee, with the $500 you already paid counted. You get it the day the sale ends, with a written statement.

Common questions

When do you get paid from an estate sale?

It depends on the company, so ask before you sign. I pay clients the day the sale ends, with a written statement of what sold and what was deducted.

How is the estate sale company's fee paid?

With me, a $500 deposit holds your dates and counts toward the flat $995 fee. The rest of the fee and the 25% of sales come out of the proceeds, so there's no second check to write.

How much would I keep from a $10,000 estate sale?

With my pricing, the total cost is $3,495. You've already paid $500, so $2,995 comes out of the proceeds and you're paid $7,005 the day the sale ends.
