How it works

From first call to a cleared-out house

Be as hands-on or hands-off as you like. Most clients aren't at the sale at all.

I take on only 2 to 3 sales a month, so every sale gets my personal attention. Spring and summer dates go first.

  1. Free walkthrough

    We walk through the home together, free of charge. I'll ask about your deadline and give you an honest estimate of what the sale could bring, including whether a sale makes sense at all.

  2. Plan, contract and deposit

    We set sale dates that work back from your deadline and put everything in a written contract. A $500 deposit holds your dates, and it counts toward the fee.

  3. Pricing and setup

    I research and price every item using recent sold listings, then set everything out so it's easy to shop. Sales are usually held inside the home; a garage or yard sale works too.

  4. Advertising

    I promote the sale on multiple platforms and to my sale-alert list, so collectors and serious buyers show up.

  5. Sale days

    My team and I handle customers, checkout and coordination from open to close. You don't need to be there.

  6. Paid and cleared

    You're paid the day the sale ends, with a written statement of what sold and what was deducted. Anything left is kept, donated or removed at no charge, your choice. Hauling away large furniture is an optional $295, and you can decide once the sale is over.

FAQ

Questions people ask before booking

Don't see yours? Call (814) 527-1079 or send a message.

How much does it cost?

A flat $995 fee plus 25% of total sales. Pricing, staging, advertising, running the sale and removing leftovers are all included, and the walkthrough before you book is free. The one optional extra is $295 to haul away large furniture. See what estate sales cost in Erie.

When do I pay?

A $500 deposit when you book holds your sale dates, and it counts toward the $995. Everything else, the rest of the fee and the 25%, comes out of the sale proceeds, so there's no second check to write.

When do I get paid?

The day the sale ends, with a written statement of what the sale brought in and what was deducted.

Do I need to be there during the sale?

No. Most clients aren't. I handle setup, pricing, customers, checkout and cleanup.

Are you insured? Do you use a contract?

Yes to both. Every sale has a written contract, and the business is insured.

Is there a minimum sale size?

No. But because the $995 is a flat fee, it's a bigger share of a small sale. I'll give you a realistic estimate at the walkthrough, and if a managed sale won't leave you enough, I'll tell you so.

Is the sale held inside or outside?

Usually inside the home, which works year-round. A garage or yard sale is an option too, especially if you're still living in the house.

What happens to things that don't sell?

You decide. Keep them, donate them, or have them removed at no charge. Large furniture is the one exception: hauling it away is an optional $295, and you don't have to decide until the sale ends.

Do you buy estates or items outright?

No. I run the sale for you, so the money from what sells goes to you instead of to a buyer's profit.

What if it rains?

Weather only matters for outdoor sales. If severe weather forces a cancellation before the sale starts, you get a 90% refund of fees paid, or you can put them toward a new date. The other 10% covers planning and advertising already done. Whenever possible, I'll reschedule rather than cancel.

How do you price items?

With current market data, recent sold listings (not asking prices) and years of buying and selling experience. Read more in how I price items.

What kinds of items sell best?

Tools, collectibles, LEGO, Star Wars, sports and trading cards, video games, hunting and fishing gear, Harley-Davidson merchandise, vintage clothing, antiques and household goods all do well.

What areas do you serve?

Erie County, including Erie, Millcreek, Harborcreek, North East, Fairview, Girard, Edinboro and Corry. Larger sales outside the county are considered case by case. See estate sales in Erie and estate sales in North East.

How far ahead should I book?

As early as you can. I take on 2 to 3 sales a month, and spring and summer dates fill first.

Why not just run the sale myself?

You can, but pricing, advertising, setup, staffing and cleanup take far more time than most people expect. I handle all of it, and careful pricing and promotion help the sale bring in more.
