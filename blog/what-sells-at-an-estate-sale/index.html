What sells

What actually sells at an estate sale, ranked

From the sales I run in Erie County: what collectors line up for, what sells steadily, what needs the right price, and what to plan for.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 11, 2026About 4 minutes to read

Every house is different, so I never promise what a sale will bring before I've walked through it. But pricing every item at my sales from recent sold listings, I see the same patterns again and again. Here's the general order, from the categories buyers drive across the county for, down to the ones you should plan around.

Top tier: collectors show up for these

  • Collectibles and toys. Star Wars, LEGO, sports and trading cards, video games. My Star Wars collector sale in North East drew a crowd through heavy rain and wind, and brought in more than $16,000.
  • Tools. Hand tools, power tools, toolboxes and shop equipment. They draw tradespeople and hobbyists alike.
  • Hunting and fishing gear. Rods, reels, packs, calls, boots and clothing, especially from brands people know.
  • Harley-Davidson gear. Leather jackets, riding gear and branded collectibles all have a following.

These are the items that get people out of bed early on day one. Because collectors are searching for them, I advertise them by name and price them from what buyers are actually paying, not from what someone is asking online.

Second tier: steady sellers

  • Jewelry and watches. From costume pieces to fine jewelry, they sell when they're sorted and displayed well.
  • Antiques and vintage. Furniture, glassware, signs and anything with a story.
  • Holiday décor. Christmas decorations in particular are steady sellers, so keep those boxes for the sale.

Third tier: sells when it's priced right

  • Kitchen and dining. Dishes, cookware, small appliances.
  • Everyday furniture. Dressers, tables, chairs and beds.
  • Lamps and décor.

There's always a buyer for everyday household goods, but only at the right price. This is where careful pricing and a good discount schedule matter. At my sales, everything is 50% off on the last day, which moves a lot of this tier.

Bottom tier: hard to sell, so plan for it

  • Tube TVs
  • Encyclopedias
  • Big TV cabinets and entertainment centers
  • Pianos

These rarely sell, even at a low price. That's fine, as long as there's a plan. With my sales, anything left over is kept, donated or removed at no charge, your choice. Hauling away large furniture is an optional $295, and you can decide once the sale is over and you see what's left.

What this means for your sale

  • Don't throw anything out first. What looks like junk is often what collectors want. Read the 3 mistakes families make.
  • Keep the best pieces for the sale. The truck, the tools or the collection bring buyers in, and they help everything else sell.
  • Check the garage, basement and attic. That's where a lot of the top tier hides.

Common questions

What sells best at estate sales?

Collectibles and toys, tools, hunting and fishing gear, and Harley-Davidson gear consistently draw the most buyers at my sales in Erie County. Jewelry, antiques and holiday décor are steady sellers too.

What doesn't sell at estate sales?

Tube TVs, encyclopedias, big TV cabinets and pianos are the hardest to sell. Plan for them up front: at my sales, leftovers are kept, donated or removed free, and large furniture removal is an optional $295.

Should I sell the valuable items before the estate sale?

Usually not. The best pieces are what bring buyers to the sale, and once they're there, they buy everything else too.
