What does an estate sale company do?

What happens from the first call to an empty house, and how companies get paid.

← All posts
By Dorian BaileyPublished November 14, 2026About 3 minutes to read

Short answer: an estate sale company sells the contents of a house for you. It prices everything, sets the house up so it's easy to shop, advertises the sale, runs the sale days and handles the money. A good one also clears out what's left. You pay a fee or a commission out of the proceeds.

What happens, start to finish

Here's how it works with me, from the first call to an empty house:

  1. Free walkthrough. I walk through the house with you and give you an honest estimate, including whether a sale makes sense at all.
  2. Dates and a written contract. Fees and sale dates go in writing before anything happens. A $500 deposit holds your dates and counts toward the fee.
  3. Pricing and setup. I research and price every item, and set the house up so buyers can shop it.
  4. Advertising. I promote the sale on multiple platforms and to my sale-alert list.
  5. Sale days. My helpers and I run the customers and the checkout. You don't need to be there.
  6. Payment. You're paid the day the sale ends, with a written statement of what sold and what was deducted.
  7. An empty house. Leftovers are kept, donated or removed free. Hauling away large furniture is an optional $295.

How estate sale companies get paid

Most charge a commission on what sells. In Erie, that's commonly 35% to 50% of total sales, and some add fees for cleanout or advertising. I charge a flat $995 plus 25% of sales, with nothing added on top. Here's the side-by-side math.

What a good company won't do

  • Leave you guessing about the money. You should get a set payday and a written statement.
  • Surprise you with fees. Ask for the all-in cost before you sign.
  • Buy your items cheaply and resell them. I don't buy estates or items outright. I run the sale for you, so the money from what sells goes to you.

When you need one

Most families call an estate sale company after a parent passes, before a move to a smaller home or senior community, or when a house has to be empty by a closing or listing date. If the house is mostly empty, a cleanout or an auction may fit better. Here's how to tell which one fits.
